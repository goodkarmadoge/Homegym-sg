01

Your search gives the wrong answer to the most obvious question

Costing you sales nowFreelancer, 1 to 2 days

I typed the single most natural thing a Singaporean customer would search on your site. Here's what came back.

What I typed, and what your site returnedI searched : "squat rack for small HDB room" Results : 1 product It showed me : "Vigor Titan G9 All-in-1 Trainer" which is not a squat rack Filters : category only. No price, no size, no brand.

Your search is matching words, not meaning. It doesn't know that "small HDB room" and "ideal for confined areas such as apartments" are the same idea, even though that second phrase is written in your own product description.

The bigger issue sits behind it. Go to your Power Rack page and there is no way to filter anything. You can sort by price and change how many products show per page. That's it. No filter by budget, no filter by size, no way to hide the things that are out of stock.

Put it another wayIt's a gym with no signage and nobody on the floor. Everything's there, the equipment is good, but a first-timer walks in, can't work out what's what, and leaves.
What to do

Two steps. First, switch on the filters. Your shop software can already do this and the information it needs is already sitting in your product records: price, brand, size, in stock or not. That's a configuration job, not a rebuild. Second, later on, replace the search itself with one that understands meaning rather than matching words.

Step one: freelancer, 1 to 2 days. Step two: see job 2 in section 05.

02

The site never asks the questions you always ask

Costing you sales nowProject, 3 to 4 weeks

Selling a power rack isn't like selling a t-shirt. There are hard physical limits: how high the ceiling is, how much floor there is, whether it fits in the lift, and whether there's a neighbour underneath who'll hear every rep. You know this. Your site behaves as though none of it exists.

So every single one of those conversations lands on you, over WhatsApp, one at a time. That's a ceiling on how much you can sell, and it's set by how many messages you can personally answer in a day.

The frustrating part is that the information needed to answer most of it automatically is already on your site. Your folding rack page states it's 155 by 166 by 228 cm open and folds to 72 cm. Nothing on the site uses those numbers to help anybody.

Put it another wayYou've got a gym full of good equipment and no instructor. Everyone who walks in has to find you and ask before they can do anything.
What to do

Build a short quiz that asks the six things you'd ask, then shows a specified setup with a price, a to-scale drawing of the footprint, and the warnings that apply to that person's flat. I've built you a working one. It's in section 06.

Effort: project, 3 to 4 weeks. The design and logic already exist.

03

Google has stopped trusting your prices

Costing you sales nowQuick fix, a few hours

Behind the scenes, every product page sends Google a hidden summary: the name, the price, whether it's in stock, and a date saying "this price is good until". Google uses it to show your price and stock status directly in search results.

Your "good until" date expired on 1 November 2025. Nine months ago. Every product page is telling Google that its own price information is out of date.

What your site tells Google, on the folding rack pagePrice : S$1,499 In stock : yes Price good until: 1 November 2025 ← expired 9 months ago

Two more things are broken in the same place. The "you are here" trail that shows Google how the product sits inside your shop only has one step in it, so Google can't see the path from your homepage down to the product. And the review information has an empty blank at the start of it, which makes the whole block unreliable to read.

Put it another wayIt's the expired safety inspection sticker on a piece of equipment. Nothing's actually wrong with the rack. But the inspector sees a lapsed date and stops vouching for it.
What to do

Have whoever maintains your site set that date to update itself automatically instead of being stamped once and forgotten, remove the empty blank, and make the trail show the full path. This is the cheapest fix in the whole report and it protects search rankings you've already earned.

Effort: freelancer, a few hours. Do this one first.

05

Your homepage takes five seconds to finish loading

Worth fixing soonFreelancer, several days

Your homepage downloads about 900 KB of behind-the-scenes program code, spread over 210 separate files, before it's done. For comparison, all the styling that makes the page look the way it does is 6 KB. The code outweighs the design by roughly 150 to 1.

Homepage, measured once from SingaporeSeparate files fetched : 250 Program code : 901 KB across 210 files Styling : 6 KB Finished loading after : 4.9 seconds Images : 78, and all 78 load immediately even the ones far down the page

Two separate things are going on. The code is being delivered as hundreds of small files instead of being packed into a few, which your shop software can do automatically and currently isn't. And every image on the page loads at once, including ones the visitor may never scroll to.

This matters most on a phone, on mobile data, which is where most people browse this category.

Put it another wayCarrying every plate in the gym over to the bench before you decide what you're actually lifting.
What to do

Turn on the built-in setting that packs the code into fewer files, and set images below the fold to load only when someone scrolls to them. Both are standard settings rather than custom work.

Effort: freelancer, several days including testing that nothing breaks.

Tracking found on every pageGoogle Analytics 4 : working Old Google Analytics: dead since July 2023 Google Ads tracking : working Facebook tracking : working

So it's worth confirming the new one is actually recording sales and add-to-carts properly, because if it isn't, you have no way to prove whether any of the changes in this report worked.

Remove the dead tracker, then have someone place a test order and confirm it shows up correctly in the working one.

Effort: freelancer, about an hour. Do this before anything else, so you can measure the rest.

JOB 1

The fit quiz

3 to 4 weeks · Start here

What it does for you

Six questions covering goal, type of home, floor space, ceiling height, experience and budget. Out comes a specified setup with a scale drawing of the footprint, an itemised price, and the warnings that apply to that person's flat. The customer gets a real answer at 11pm without messaging anybody.

Why it's first

It fixes the two biggest problems at once, it needs no change to your shop software, and it turns your weakest point, that nobody can work out what fits, into the thing you're known for. When they do message you, they arrive with a specification instead of "how big is your smallest rack".

How it works

  • Reads your live prices and stock
  • Saves every completed quiz as a lead
  • Opens WhatsApp with the setup already filled in
  • Works while you're asleep or on a delivery

How you'd judge it

  • Sales from quiz users against everyone else
  • Messages needed per sale, before and after
JOB 2

Search that understands meaning

2 to 3 weeks

What it does for you

Instead of matching typed words against product names, it understands what someone means. "Something for a small bedroom", "quiet enough for HDB" and "a rack that folds away" all find the right products.

The language is already in your own copy. Your folding rack description says "ideal for confined areas such as apartments, garages and small rooms". A word-matching search can't connect that to somebody typing "small HDB room". This can.

How it works

  • Reads all your descriptions, specs and reviews
  • Keeps the existing search underneath as a backstop
  • Pushes in-stock items up the results
  • Logs searches that found nothing

The hidden benefit

  • Failed searches tell you what customers want that you don't stock, which is free market research you currently throw away
JOB 3

WhatsApp first responder

4 to 6 weeks

What it does for you

Handles the messages you answer twenty times a week: is it in stock, what size is it, when can you deliver, how much is assembly, when's the showroom open. It also collects the four facts you always need, flat type, ceiling height, floor space and lift access, before you pick up the conversation.

Outside your opening hours it holds the conversation instead of leaving someone waiting until the morning.

The important limit

It should only ever quote facts from your product records, and hand straight over to you the moment anyone asks for advice, a discount, or anything about an existing order. A confidently wrong dimension costs you a wasted delivery, so it's better for it to say less.

How you'd judge it

  • How many conversations it finishes without you
  • How fast people get a first reply at night and on Sundays
  • Whether it hands over when it should, checked weekly by you reading a sample

Do this after

  • Job 1, so you already know which questions actually matter

07

Do you need a new website?

Short answer: not yet, but decide before you spend much more. Your site runs on Magento, which is powerful, heavy, and more than most one-person businesses need. Three honest options.

Option A

Keep it, fix it

Stay where you are. Do the repairs in this report, switch on the filters, add the quiz on top.

Cost
Lowest by far. Weeks, not months.
Risk
Low. Nothing moves, nothing breaks.
The catch
Magento will always be slower than it should be, and your current design limits how good the shopping experience can get.

Right if you want more revenue this quarter and can't take on a project.

Option B · What I'd recommend

New front, same engine

Keep Magento running your stock, orders and prices behind the scenes. Rebuild only the part customers see, starting with the pages that actually sell.

Cost
Middle. Roughly three months for the main pages.
Risk
Contained. Move one page type at a time. Your order system never changes, so day-to-day work carries on as normal.
The upside
The quiz, the better search and the speed all become natural rather than bolted on.

Best fit here. You keep fourteen years of products, orders and reviews, and lose the thing that's actually costing you sales.

Option C

Move to Shopify

Start again on simpler, hosted software with lots of ready-made add-ons.

Cost
Highest, and most of it hidden in moving the data across.
Risk
Highest. Your web addresses and your 1,500 reviews all have to move, and both can go wrong in ways that cost you Google traffic.
The catch
Easier to run day to day, but there's no off-the-shelf add-on that does space-fit selling. You'd still have to build the thing that makes you different.

Only if keeping Magento running has become a genuine burden on you personally.
